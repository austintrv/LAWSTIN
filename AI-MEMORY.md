## What Lawstin is
- Austin’s law-school assistant for all five Fall 2026 courses at UH Law Center: International Law (`il`), Asylum Law (`asy`), Professional Responsibility (`pr`), Civil Clinic (`clinic`), and Civil Procedure Tutoring (`tutoring`). Single user. Clinic and Tutoring workspaces hold a task list (`course_tasks`), notes, links to Austin’s Drive files, and (Clinic) an hours card. The quiz database’s existing three-course enum stays unchanged.
- Installed to the iPhone Home Screen and the Mac Dock as a full-screen web app (no browser bars). It must look and feel like a polished, company-made Apple-style app. No small explanatory helper text or captions anywhere in the UI.
- Four-choice questions; each choice has a role (`correct`, `sneaky`, `average`, `bad`) and its own explanation. Spaced review: a miss returns in 1 day; correct answers move out to 3, 7, 16, then 35 days. Practice has no fixed round: it pulls 10 questions at a time, with recall cards mixed in, and keeps going until nothing is left; leaving keeps every answer. Daily goal: 5 to 40, set by the finals plan while a final is ahead (otherwise the fixed goal, 20 by default), and held steady through the day.
- Screens (the tab set and several screens below changed in the November 2026 upgrade; the 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12 Log entries are current where they differ): Study (default tab: red italic greeting chosen once per open from ~170 lines; hero card with course filter, today block (goal ring, “N to go today”, minutes estimate, streak chip; tapping it opens the “How Lawstin works” sheet), the main button, and three rows named “Ready to review”, “Not seen yet”, “Missed topics”; Finals tiles; To do card for Clinic/Tutoring tasks due within two days; tools list: Practice exam, Flashcards, Written practice, Match, Starred; Latest update card with at most six topic bubbles and a +N bubble. The per-course list shows only after every final has passed), Courses (compact five-course directory and six tool tiles; course pages with Review cards, Practice questions, Rulebook and Written practice; task workspaces for Clinic and Tutoring), Progress (wrong-answer profile and one coverage card per course, collapsible on phone, three columns on Mac), Activity (Missed topics capped at four with a toggle, Study calendar of the last five weeks, 14-day chart, Updates feed). Settings from the red “A” avatar; Connected apps sits in its own Connections group. Quiz: back button and swipe to revisit earlier answers and explanations; tap a choice again to deselect; one consistent review card style; ✓/✗ tally, in-a-row flame from three, and today’s goal bar and count in the top bar; a tag on each practice question saying why it is shown (“New”, “Missed 2 days ago”, “Right 7 days ago”, “Guessed last time”); a five-step ladder (1d, 3d, 7d, 16d, 35d) under “Next review”; “All caught up” ring at the end. `#/q/<id>` opens one question (used by question nudges).

## Exam formats and the finals plan (Austin, 2026-11-09)
- Professional Responsibility: closed-book multiple choice. International Law: essay, printed notes allowed. Asylum Law: open-book essay. For the two essay courses Austin cares about understanding the material, not memorizing it. `EXAMS` in `src/helpers/studyEngine.tsx` records this; content and features follow it.
- Why this was built: Austin had stopped using the app because the bank was thin and uneven and nearly everything was multiple choice.
- Finals plan (`src/helpers/studySession.ts`, on by default, switch in Settings): while a final is ahead the daily goal is computed, spreading unseen questions and cards over the days left (done two days early) plus up to 10 reviews due per course, within 5 to 40. A review scheduled on or after a final returns in the last five days (`examDue`). Essay courses get an essay every other day, daily in the last week.
- Daily session: the main button mixes questions with recall cards (two questions, then a card). Cards are library flashcards plus, for Pro Res only, “State the rule” cards built from the `rule` of questions already answered (ids `rule:<question id>`, progress under `study:v1:cards:rule:…`). A typed explanation is checked by the tutor (`explain`); he still grades the card himself.
- Answer before choices (on by default, switch in Settings): practice hides the four choices until he taps Show choices; anything typed appears on the review. Never in exams.
- Written practice: 15- or 45-minute prompts with a clock. An unwritten verified hypothetical is served first and graded against its own checked analysis; otherwise the tutor writes the prompt. Every graded essay is saved (`written:v1:<id>` rows in `app_settings`) and counts as 5 answers (10 at exam length) toward the day, right in proportion to the score (`study_events` kind `written`). A score under 7 marks the topic weak for two weeks. Hypotheticals in Courses open here with “Write and grade”.
- Practice exam: Pro Res and Mixed are timed multiple choice at two minutes a question (or untimed), unseen questions first, results by topic with time per question. Intl Law and Asylum open a 45-minute essay.
- Coverage map (Progress tab): every unit taught, in class order, from `syllabus:v1:<course>` rows in `app_settings` (`{"units":[{"topic","dates","also"}]}`), marked Not covered, New, Shaky, Learning or Solid. Built 2026-11-09 from the three syllabi and the Pro Res DAY file names; Pro Res days 17 and 21 came from placeholder notes and inference, so the question task corrects rows against the DAY notes. On 2026-11-09, 9 of 14 taught Intl Law units, 5 of 12 Asylum and 8 of 14 Pro Res had no questions.

## Austin’s design preferences (from his feedback; apply to every change)
- No small explanatory helper text, captions, placeholder promises (“coming soon”, “will live here”), drafting or process notes, or references to source documents in the UI or in study content.
- No marketing slogans or hero banners (“Five courses. One workspace.”, “Your law school. In one place.”) and no institution labels in chrome (“UH Law Center”).
- No plain paragraphs of text for updates or summaries; show them as visual, tappable elements (notification cards, colored topic bubbles, chips).
- It must behave like a native Apple app: no pinch zoom, no long-press text selection on controls, no content that visibly swaps after appearing (the greeting is chosen once, after data loads).
- Consistent typography and spacing: one card style, weights 400 (body) and 600 (labels, primary lines, headings under 22px), 700 only for titles and big numbers 22px and up; nothing at 500 or 650. One small label style, no randomly light or dark text, even gaps between boxes.
- Full-screen views (quiz, Written practice, Ask, flashcards, reader) share one header: title centered, Close (36px gray circle, 44px tap area) on the right, Back or Star on the left. Settings is a pushed page with “Back”. Buttons: red primary, gray secondary.
- Quiz: always allow going back to earlier questions and explanations; no forced rounds; leaving keeps his place.
- Answer choices must not reveal the correct one through extra detail, length, or rule-tracking qualifiers.
- Joy matters: witty greetings, smooth animations, satisfying feedback. He wants to love the app so he studies more.
- Desktop should be its own design, not the phone layout stretched.
- On a Mac (1000px and wider) every full-screen view fills the window, not a phone-width column: quiz, review, results, Missed lesson, Written practice, Ask, flashcards, every study-material format, and Settings. Study views use two columns up to `--study-width` (1400px, `src/base.css`); check new screens at 1440px wide before publishing.
- No Print buttons anywhere (Austin removed them 2026-10-29).
